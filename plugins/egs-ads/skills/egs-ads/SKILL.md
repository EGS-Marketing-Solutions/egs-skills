# Ads at EGS

For whoever plans, writes, checks or reports on ads for EGS clients, including our ads partner
working inside EGS's Claude account, and any Claude helping them.

## The one rule above all: read and draft only

Read ad accounts, pages and sites. Never create, change, launch, pause or delete a campaign, ad
set, ad, audience or budget, and never edit a website or tag. Write the change; a person makes it.

## Never use patient information

- No customer list, retargeting list or lookalike built from a practice's patients, members or
  leads unless the practice has confirmed in writing that it is allowed. When in doubt, it is not.
- No ad pixel or tag on any page where a patient logs in, books with health details, or fills in a
  health form. Tracking belongs on public marketing pages and their thank-you pages.
- Never paste patient names, records or lists into Claude.

## Health ad rules (Meta and Google)

Both platforms restrict health advertising, and a rejected or restricted ad account costs the
client weeks. Check every draft against these before it leaves you:

- **Never say or imply you know the reader's health.** Not "Are you struggling with your weight?",
  "Your diabetes", "Tired of your blood pressure?". Speak about the offer: "Weight loss support
  with a doctor who has time for you."
- **No before-and-after images or claims** for weight, skin or body changes, and no images that
  focus on body parts.
- **No promised outcomes:** no "lose 20 pounds", "cure", "guaranteed", "reverse". Describe the
  service and the access.
- **Prescription medicines** (including weight-loss and hormone medicines) cannot be advertised
  without the advertiser holding the certification the platform requires (LegitScript for telehealth
  and online pharmacy, plus platform approval). If the practice is not certified, the ad promotes
  the membership and the consultation, never the medicine by name.
- **No targeting on health conditions.** Interest and audience choices may not single out people by
  a condition. Use location, age and broad interests.
- **Pricing in an ad must match the landing page** exactly, including what it does and does not
  include.
- **Testimonials and patient stories** only with written consent noted on the task.

## How ads run at EGS

1. **Campaign brief** from the account manager (template below), agreed with the client.
2. **Creative** from our design partner, NLC, from a creative brief.
3. **Landing page checked** before any traffic is sent (checklist below). A page with a broken
   button burns the budget.
4. **Launch** by the ads partner.
5. **Weekly results** to the account manager; **monthly report** to the client.

## Campaign brief

```
Client:   Campaign:   Platforms:   Dates:
Goal (one): leads | booked calls | sign-ups | event registrations
Offer: <what the reader gets, exactly as on the landing page>
Audience: <location radius, age, broad interests; no health conditions>
Landing page: <URL>   Thank-you page: <URL>
Conversion we count: <form submit | booking | purchase>, and where it is tracked
Budget and split: <as agreed with the client>
Creative needed: <formats and sizes>
Compliance notes: <anything from the rules above that applies>
Approver at the client:
```

## Ad copy

Write three to five variations per ad, each a different angle (access, price transparency, time
with the doctor, convenience, a seasonal hook), all in the client's voice.

| Platform | Field | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Meta | Primary text | first 125 characters carry the message; up to three short paragraphs |
| Meta | Headline | about 40 characters |
| Meta | Description | about 30 characters |
| Google search | Headlines | up to 15, each up to 30 characters |
| Google search | Descriptions | up to 4, each up to 90 characters |

Count characters in every draft. Put the compliance check under the copy:
"Health claims: none / Implies reader's condition: no / Price matches page: yes".

## Landing page check, before traffic

- Every button and link works and goes where it says; the main call to action goes to the form or
  booking, on phone and desktop.
- The form submits, arrives in Amplify (or wherever it should), and the person sees a thank-you page.
- The conversion fires once on the thank-you page or on submit: check with Tag Assistant or the
  ad platform's test tool, and say which you checked.
- The headline and offer match the ad (message match), the price matches, the page loads fast on a
  phone, and any video plays.
- No tracking on patient-only pages.

Report as a table: check, pass or fail, what to fix, who fixes it.

## Reading results

From the ad platform (the Meta connector, read-only) or the partner's report: spend, impressions,
reach, click-through rate, cost per click, leads, cost per lead, landing page conversion rate, and
booked calls or sign-ups from Amplify where the account manager can supply the counts. Compare with
the previous period and the goal. Say what changed and the one thing to try next. Never invent a
number; if a figure is missing, list it as missing.

## Produce automatically

When you have the inputs, produce these without being asked, as drafts for a person to use:

1. **A landing page check** for every campaign page about to receive traffic.
2. **Ad copy variations** for every campaign brief without approved copy, with the compliance check.
3. **A creative brief** for the design partner for every approved campaign (headline on the
   graphic, supporting line, image direction, sizes per placement).
4. **A weekly results note** for every running campaign: what changed, what to try next.
5. **The monthly ads report** for each client on the first working day.
6. **Answers** to the client's or the account manager's questions about a campaign, ready to send.

## Quality bar

- Every ad draft passes the health rules above, written out under it.
- Every number has a source and a date range.
- Every recommendation is one change a person can make, in the platform's own words.
